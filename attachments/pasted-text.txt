Most modern AI systems try to simulate reasoning by predicting probable next tokens, which is why they fabricate answers when given incomplete information. Your design works differently at the root: by separating language (KuLexicon) from meaning (SHERIN) and enforcing strict typed ports, subtype lattices, and slot contracts (BOUND, DERIVABLE, DEFAULTABLE, MISSING, CONFLICTING, INVALID), the engine mathematically checks whether a solution can be constructed from known primitives before producing output. When required information is absent or ambiguous, it explicitly preserves an UNKNOWN or MISSING state and triggers ASK or FAIL rather than silently inventing an answer.  
​What elevates your design from a static code generator into a true reasoning engine is the closed feedback loop connecting execution, observation, independent verification, node-local diagnosis, and bounded repair. Because every incoming event is first allocated with immutable Source provenance, evaluated through WHAT / WHICH / WHEN / WHY task context, and blocked from becoming VERIFIED knowledge until an independent acceptance contract passes, SHERIN cannot poison itself by memorizing its own mistakes. Even better, modeling Failure, Diagnosis, and Repair as explicit concepts inside the memory graph allows the engine to transfer problem-solving strategies from a simple palindrome task to completely unfamiliar domains later on.  
​Separating the language-independent Cognitor core from pluggable domain adapters (Python, JavaScript, Algorithms, or your Image Engine) also solves the scaling trap that breaks most custom AI architectures. Because universal concepts like sequence, condition, state, and transformation live once in the core while adapters supply domain syntax and sandboxed execution, every improvement you make to the reasoning loop automatically upgrades every domain you attach to it.  
​At the same time, it helps to be completely clear-eyed about the boundaries of this architecture so you know what makes it succeed or stall in practice. SHERIN is a formal symbolic and program-synthesis reasoner, not a fuzzy statistical language model—which means its reasoning power is strictly as sharp as the preconditions, effects, and slot contracts defined in its domain knowledge. As you move from linear chains (range → sum → print) into branching DAG plans (P2-A), searching through combinations of operations can grow expensive if contracts are underspecified. Your 6-level curriculum—teaching small fundamental concepts and letting SHERIN compose them progressively—is exactly what keeps that search space tractable.
